# Phase 2 Mechanism-Selection Review

## Purpose

Phase 2 uses the existing active taxonomy as an annotation instrument rather than silently expanding it. This review records why the pilot should test those labels and where annotator disagreement would be informative.

The review is informed by the Phase 1 research reference corpus, including the project preprint *The Antipodean Jester* and the documented comedy/reference sources. Those materials are research references and hypothesis sources, not redistributable dialogue and not cultural ground truth.

## Active Phase 2 taxonomy

The pilot interface exposes the same active labels already defined by the Phase 1 schema:

- `understatement`
- `sarcasm`
- `irony`
- `deadpan`
- `affectionate_insult`
- `inverse_praise`
- `profanity_non_hostile`
- `discourse_marker`
- `self_deprecation`
- `absurdist_escalation`
- `relational_teasing`
- `tall_poppy_humour`
- `ambiguous_address`
- `literal`
- `unknown`

Multiple labels may be selected. `unknown` remains available when the active taxonomy does not justify a more specific mechanism.

## Why the pilot emphasises contrasts

The 60-item pilot pack contains 30 same-utterance context contrasts. This is intentional because the Phase 1 invariants predict that lexical form alone will often be insufficient.

The pilot is designed to expose annotation difficulty around several recurring families:

### Discourse markers and address terms

Items using forms such as `yeah nah`, `nah yeah`, `righto`, `no worries`, `mate`, and `old mate` are included in contrasting contexts. The purpose is not to establish phrase-to-intent rules. It is to test whether annotators can apply context-dependent interpretations consistently without converting cultural tendencies into deterministic dictionaries.

### Understatement and literal controls

Several utterances appear once in an extreme context and once in a comparatively literal or proportionate context. This tests whether the annotation guide makes the distinction between lexical mildness and pragmatic understatement usable in practice.

### Sarcasm, inverse praise, and sincere praise

Positive surface forms such as `Top job`, `Brilliant`, `Nice one`, and `Perfect` appear in both successful and failure contexts. These items test whether annotators distinguish surface sentiment from pragmatic sentiment while preserving cases where the surface-positive reading is genuinely sincere.

### Relationship-conditioned insult and teasing

The pilot contrasts close-friend, workplace, and stranger contexts for superficially negative or teasing forms. These items are intended to probe AU-HUMOUR-001, AU-HUMOUR-002, AU-HUMOUR-003, and AU-HUMOUR-008 without assuming that profanity or teasing is automatically benign.

### Tall-poppy and status-calibration hypotheses

Items such as `Big shot now, are ya?`, `Settle down, superstar.`, `Yeah, we get it, you're a genius.`, and `Don't let it go to your head.` are deliberately included in contexts that separate playful status deflation, reaction to boasting, and potentially unfair status suppression.

This treats tall-poppy humour as a research hypothesis about status-sensitive pragmatics, not as a claim that Australians universally dislike success. A useful Phase 2 outcome would be evidence about whether annotators can reliably distinguish:

- affectionate or egalitarian teasing;
- scepticism toward unsupported status claims;
- ordinary congratulations with deflationary humour;
- hostile or belittling treatment of demonstrated competence.

No new invariant is asserted at this stage.

## Candidate mechanisms deliberately not promoted

The Phase 1 reference corpus also records candidate concepts such as institutional mimicry, adversarial questioning, performed persona, unreliable authority, frame switching, and pragmatic misunderstanding.

They remain research candidates rather than active Phase 2 schema labels. Promoting them before the pilot would make the taxonomy larger before there is evidence that annotators can apply the existing labels consistently.

## What Phase 2 should decide

After real annotations are collected, mechanism selection should be reviewed using:

1. frequency of `unknown` selections;
2. pairwise mechanism-set overlap;
3. recurring annotator notes describing a missing concept;
4. confusion between neighbouring labels such as sarcasm, irony, and inverse praise;
5. whether `tall_poppy_humour` is applied consistently enough to remain useful;
6. whether any label encourages stereotyping or fixed phrase heuristics.

The annotation guide should then be revised from observed pilot evidence. Until that happens, this document records a pilot hypothesis and review plan, not a validated taxonomy.
